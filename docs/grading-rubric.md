# Grading rubric (collector track)

Condensed from AbeBooks' grading guide, the IOBA condition definitions, and common dealer
practice (Very Fine Books, Townsend, Tolkien Library guide). Grades are absolute: a 1919 book
and a 2019 book in "Very Good" look alike for their materials. Normal aging (light toning,
slight edge wear) is already allowed for in the lower-middle grades; it is not a reason to bump
a grade up.

## Terms
| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| As New | Perfect, as published. No defects. Rarely right for anything handled. |
| Fine (F) | Close to As New; may show slight signs of handling. No defects worth listing. |
| Near Fine (NF) | Between Fine and VG; one or two very small defects, named. |
| Very Good (VG) | Shows some wear, not torn. Every defect named. Typical clean used copy. |
| Good (G) | Average used, worn copy; all pages present. Every defect named. |
| Fair | Worn; may lack endpapers, half-title etc. (must be stated). Binding may be loose. |
| Poor | Reading copy only; heavy damage, soiling, loose text. Missing pages stated. |

- `+` / `-` allowed (e.g. VG-). Use sparingly.
- **Jacket graded separately**: "Very Good in a Good dust jacket". No jacket: say "no dust jacket"
  if one was issued.
- **Ex-library** always stated in the grade line, whatever the condition ("ex-library, Good").
  Book club editions stated.
- **Always disclose**: foxing, toning, soiling, stains (damp/water), mould, odour if noted by seller,
  inscriptions, bookplates, stamps, owner names, underlining, loose/cracked hinges, shaken text,
  missing/torn pages or plates, repairs, rebinding, price-clipped jackets, fading of spine.
- Do not disclose what isn't there. Do not use "a few", "some" vaguely when a count is visible.

## Fields recorded (hidden from buyers)
jacket, boards/covers, spine, corners/edges, hinges/joints, text block (page edges, toning,
foxing), plates/illustrations, marks (names, stamps, plates, inscriptions), repairs. Each: term,
note, photo ID. "Nothing notable" fields are omitted from the prose.

## Buyer-facing paragraph (rendered by code)
`Condition: <Grade>.` then positives first (what is bright, tight, complete), then wear from
outside in (cloth, spine, corners, edges, page block, prelims, plates), then one closing verdict
sentence ("A sound, honest copy..."). One paragraph, plain words, no grading table.

## eBay condition field
Fine/NF/VG -> Very Good (4000); Good -> Good (5000); Fair/Poor -> Acceptable (6000).
